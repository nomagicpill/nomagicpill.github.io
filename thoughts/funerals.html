Total Value of Eulogies

In my culture, eulogies are only written for great people or great friends. Obituaries—very matter-of-fact biographies with little to no praise—are much more common. I suspect this is because there are few great people out there, because if there were people would make more of an effort to eulogize them.

From this, it can be argued that a person's value can be measured by the total value of their posthumous praise, calculated by:

  1. How many praises they receive (e.g., how many blog eulogies were written?)
  2. How high the praise is
  3. How much effort the praiser went through to publish said praise (e.g., is the eulogy long and well-written?)
