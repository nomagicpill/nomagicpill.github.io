The Spectrum of Seriousness

Seriousness exists on a spectrum. In the context of achieving a goal, one end of the spectrum is complete unseriousness, i.e., the player doesn't do anything to further their goal. On the other end is obsessiveness, where the player does the bare minimum in other parts of their life to maximize progress towards their goal—they may lose friends, sufer health issues, or enter financial straits in their pursuit.

But actual seriousness (AS) and claimed seriousness (CS) can be, and often are, two very different points on the spectrum. Economists call AS a revealed preference and CS a stated preference.

I suspect CS follows a normal distribution, while AS follows a distribution that is skewed right (i.e., the bulk of people have below-average AS) when compared to CS, i.e., most people are not as serious at their goals as they claim. This is because most people's claims do not match up to their actuals for a few potential reasons:

  • They know they're not that serious, but claim to be so for status, etc.
  • Their calibration is off, and they think their AS equals their CS
