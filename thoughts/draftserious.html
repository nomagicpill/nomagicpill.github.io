The Spectrum of Seriousness

Seriousness exists on a spectrum. In the context of achieving a goal, one end of the spectrum is complete unseriousness, i.e., the player doesn't do anything to further their goal. On the other end is obsessiveness, where the player does the bare minimum in other parts of their life to maximize progress towards their goal—they may lose friends, suffer health issues, or enter financial straits in their pursuit of greatness.

But actual seriousness (AS) and claimed seriousness (CS) can be, and often are, two very different points on the spectrum. Economists call AS a revealed preference and CS a stated preference. I'll call them AS and CS because it's a bit of a subset of preference theory. It also makes it look like I came up with something original.

I suspect CS follows a normal distribution, while AS follows a distribution that is skewed right (i.e., the bulk of people have below-average AS) when compared to CS, i.e., most people are not as serious at their goals as they claim. This is because most people's claims do not match up to their actuals for a few potential reasons:

  • They know they're not that serious, but claim to be so for status, fitting in, etc.
    • Ex: Lydia doesn't like music that much, but it's gotten her respect, friends, and a paycheck, so she continues doing the bare minimum, which is far less than what she could actually be doing if she truly cared about music
  • Their calibration is off, and they think their AS equals their CS, i.e., they don't know that they could be more serious.
    • Ex: Sharon says she is serious about music, putting her time and money where her mouth is by spending $1000 on a violin and practing an hour or two a day. In reality, strong violinists easily spend five figures on their violin and up to five hours per day practicing. Sharon thought she was nearing the ceiling, when in reality she's nowhere close.

The rest of this will explore how to get AS to equal CS, whether it's increasing or decreasing it.

Examples

Here are some examples of potential lack of seriousness:

  • Krista doesn't use LLMs because "they're just stochastic parrots". They instead choose to write everything by hand. They refuse to even test a model a model on principle and scowl at colleagues using it. Meanwhile, her colleagues use a hybrid approach, getting LLMs to do the bulk of the work while they check its accuracy and completeness and speeding up their deployment time immensely.
  • Olga doesn't have a way to measure cycling power because "it's too expensive" and "RPE-based training works just fine" and "analyzing power data is too time-consuming". While she's busy training on feel and not making much progress, her competitors are using data to guide their training, making leaps and bounds in both fitness and recovery. (RPE 7 will always feel RPE 7, so if you're not recovering enough you can't tell because RPE 7 = RPE 7.)
  • Francesca is trying to lose weight by eating less. But counting calories and weighing food is "too inconvenient", so she doesn't do it. Using a GLP-1 is also "cheating". Her friends, on the other hand, are serious: Eliot obsessively counts and tracks calories, taking care to measure ingredients out carefully and asking restaurants for nutrition info if they have it; Andris got prescribed a GLP-1
