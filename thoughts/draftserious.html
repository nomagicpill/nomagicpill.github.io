The Spectrum of Seriousness

In the context of achieving a goal, one end of the seriousness spectrum is complete unseriousness, where the player doesn't do anything to further their goal. On the other end is obsessiveness, where the player does the bare minimum in other parts of their life to maximize progress towards their goal—they may lose friends, suffer health issues, or enter financial straits in their pursuit of glory and greatness.

But actual seriousness (AS) and claimed seriousness (CS) can be, and often are, two very different points on the spectrum. Economists call AS a revealed preference and CS a stated preference. I'll call them AS and CS because it's feels like a subset of preference theory. It also makes it sound like I came up with something original.

I suspect CS follows a normal distribution (i.e., most people claim they care about some things an average amount), while AS follows a distribution that is skewed right (i.e., the bulk of people have below-average AS) when compared to CS. This mismatch means most people are not as serious towards their goals as they claim. This can be caused by a few reasons:

  • They know they're not that serious, but claim to be so for status, fitting in, etc.
    • Ex: Lydia doesn't like music that much, but it's gotten her respect, friends, and a paycheck, so she continues doing the bare minimum, which is far less than what she could actually be doing if she truly cared about music.
  • Their calibration is off, and they think their AS equals their CS, i.e., they don't know that they could be more serious.
    • Ex: Sharon says she is serious about music, putting her time and money where her mouth is by spending $1000 on a violin and practing an hour or two a day. In reality, professional violinists easily spend five figures on their violin and up to four hours per day practicing. Sharon thought she was nearing the ceiling, when in reality she's nowhere close.

Both of these are issues. Intentionally lying to others corrupts the soul. How else could that time be spent if the honest path was taken? The miscalibration leaves plenty of progress on the table. What if Sharon learned that there are still returns to be had between the one- and four-hour practice marks? That's 15+ hours a week she's missing out on!

The point of this is to help the wannabe-serious person realize they're not as serious as they could be, and also as a reference for me to see how much more I could be doing.

Questions to Help Determine Seriousness

Any "no" answers to the following questions probably means you aren't as serious as you could be.

Are You Incurring a Cost?

Cost being time, money, social life, opportunity, etc. Revealed preferences strike again, and if someone is serious about something, they should be willing to pay for it in proporition to how serious they are. Are you spending money towards it? Is your free time being taken up by it? Are you sacrificing social and romantic opportunities to pursue it?

Are You Doing the Thing?

No, like actually doing it. Getting better at writing requires writing, cycling requires cycling. Fuckarounditis is a serious disease that afflicts countless serious people every year without them realizing it. The SAID principle is real.

Are You Learning and Applying Those Lessons?

Are you reviewing your work and trying to find ways to get better? Are you doing post-mortems, analyzing them for deficiencies, then finding ways to patch those holes quickly so the same mistake isn't made again? Are you seeking out ways to learn, whether online, in person, through books and film and podcasts and interviews? Are you actively trying to learn? Not just sitting there letting the knowledge go over you, but actively thinking, generating new ideas, testing them, refining them? Are you seeking out failures to learn from?

Are You Seeking Feedback to Get Better?

Are you asking others to give you raw, honest, constructive feedback and really accepting it and making the necessary corrections? Are you even open to feedback? Are you increasing your surface area for feedback to reach you, and then following up when it does to get even more?

Are You Competitive?

Are you trying to become the best? Are you trying to outwork your competitors? Do you have a way to rank yourself, whether on an absolute scale or against others?d

If a Stranger Watched You For a Week, Would They Think You're Serious About This?

Or describe your day to a stranger in 15-minute increments and ask them "what do you think I'm serious about?". If their answer is different from yours, think about why that may be. Does your time actually reflect your claimed seriousness?

Are You Looking at Serious People and Trying to Mimic Them?

Look at people you consider to be serious. How is their life different from yours? Are they really built different, or are they doing things you aren't willing to? Kobe Bryant practiced basketball for hours every day. Startup founders and their teams work 100 hours a week in pursuit of their mission.

Examples

Here are some fictitious examples of both seriousness and lack thereof across different fields:

  • Krista doesn't use LLMs for coding because "they're just stochastic parrots". They instead choose to write everything by hand. They refuse to even test a model on principle and scowl at colleagues using it. Meanwhile, her colleagues use a hybrid approach, getting LLMs to do the bulk of the work while they check its accuracy and completeness, ultimately speeding up their deployment time immensely.
  • Olga doesn't have a way to measure cycling power because "it's too expensive" and "RPE-based training works just fine" and "analyzing power data is too time-consuming". While she's busy training on feel and not making much progress, her competitors are using data to guide their training, making leaps and bounds in both fitness and recovery. (RPE 7 will always feel RPE 7, so if you're not recovering enough you can't tell because RPE 7 = RPE 7.)
  • Francesca is trying to lose weight by eating less. But counting calories and weighing food is "too inconvenient", so she doesn't do it. Using a GLP-1 is also "cheating". Her friends, on the other hand, are more involved: Eliot obsessively counts and tracks calories, taking care to measure ingredients out carefully and asking restaurants for nutrition info if they have it; Andris got prescribed a GLP-1 and follows the prescriptions. Both of them shed pounds by the week.
  • Todd wants to spend less time on his phone, so he sets a non-password-protected screen time limit on the most distracting apps. This doesn't change anything—he blazes right past the gates through self-persuasion and habit. Scott takes a different approach: he purchases a phone lock box, removes the addictive apps, makes it greyscale, and stores his screen time password in an online locker that's guarded by 60 minutes of inconvenient, annoying work.
  • Whitney is a competitive athlete who wants to up her game, and everything points to needing to spend more time on recovery. She starts to eat a bit cleaner, get more sleep, and be less stressed. She makes some progress. Andris is in the same boat, but puts more effort into his recovery: he tracks macros to ensure he's hitting what he needs to, ensures he fuels within 60 minutes of the end of his workout, follows sleep hygiene practices, regularly foam rolls, prehabs his various joints and muscles, and actively recovers insead of taking an entire day off.
