The Spectrum of Seriousness

Seriousness exists on a spectrum. In the context of achieving a goal, one end of the spectrum is complete unseriousness, i.e., not doing anything to further the goal. On the other end is obsessiveness, where the player does the bare minimum in other parts of their life to maximize progress towards the goal—they may lose friends, sufer health issues, or enter financial straits in their pursuit.

But actual seriousness and claimed seriousness can be two very different points on the spectrum. Economists call the actual seriousness a revealed preference and the claimed seriousness a stated preference.
