The Current State of Affairs

Within the division at my public company, managers get promoted one of two ways, depending on their soon-to-be level:

  • Manager of individual contributors (MIC): Interviewed by panel of MICs and manager of MoICs (MoM), then the MoM selects the best one with input from the MICs.
  • Manager of managers (MoM): Future manager (Mo...M) decides singlehandedly, sometimes with input from others.

Why is this the way? A few potential reasons in no particular order, with some refutations in parentheses:

  1. "We've always done it this way" (doesn't mean that the process can't be improved)
  2. "The hiring manager knows best" (do they?)
  3. "The hiring manager is the one they'll be working for, so they get the final decision" (a bit more fair, but still not necessarily the best decision)
  4. "Big companies must do things the bad way, this is a bad way, therefore we must do it this way" (company's syllogism)

The wrong side of the manager is being considered here. Instead, it should be the employees working for the new manager that should be interviewing and making the final decision. This creates a pyramid of-sorts, where individual contributors (ICs) vote for their immediate manager and even more ICs vote for their manager's manager. Eventually the entire company votes for the CEO.

[problem with current method is they can be cutthroat and good for company short term but bad long term]

The New Styles

I propose two new methods for deciding on new managers.

Popular Vote

A population—either everyone within the organization or just those who would be affected by the new manager—vote yay or nay. A simple majority will accept the candidate.

All votes are anonymous.

Representatives

Representatives are voted on by the entire organization's population, then are the sole ones to conduct interviews and hire.

All representative votes are anonymous.
