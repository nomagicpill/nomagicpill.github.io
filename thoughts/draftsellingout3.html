Avoid becoming a slave to the stats.

Thesis

It's difficult to receive external feedback and not change direction in some form. The desire to impress others, to make money, to gain status, to feel validated, to see numbers go up is incredibly strong, enough to convince us of or help justify things we didn't previously believe, thought were evil, etc. What may have started as a hobby in a creative art has turned into an exercise of figuring out how to satisfy some external goal—money, status, validation—as dictated by others. The next project, one that brought excitement and interest, is now being tweaked to get just a bit more exposure, a bit more agreement from the audience, a bit more revenue.

"But the Market Is Telling Me Something!"

Sure it is in some sense, but most side-hustle creatives aren't doing what they do to satisfy some niche market: they're doing it for themselves and because they like it. A noble pursuit the cost incurred with no expectation of reward at the end—love of the game only. (This is not to say that pursuing something for money isn't noble—there are still many sacrifices that must be taken. This is just a different type of nobility.)

Markets corrupt. Despite being one of the best things ever, free markets undeniably change behaviors as people respond to feedback and incentives in an effort to gain resources. This often comes at the expense of the seller's original creative vision and intention. They sell out in an effort to satisfy their fans, both new and old, perverting their soul in the process without realizing it.

Alternatives

So what's the alternative? Going full hermit mode in a cabin in Montana doesn't offer a chance to test ideas in the arena of the internet, an important part of getting better at anything. Companies can't build, test, and sell a product in secret if they want to make a better product: they need feedback in the form of sales, not-sales, and comments from consumers. The same principle applies to the creative arts. Writing, for example, should be put out into the world so other can read it and either tear it to shreds or go on Substack and call it the best thing they've ever read without offering any other commentary. The holes can then slowly get patched and certain styles reinforc... wait a sec! Was that style really you? Weren't you just testing that phrase to see what happened? You didn't think it would actually take off!

The key here is weighting and filtering. Filter out or weight all the buffoons, the charlatans, the anons, the people whose opinions aren't that valuable. Focus on the high-quality people of the creative world you inhabit and work to gain their attention and, ultimately, respect. One shoutout by a Scott Alexander is worth 100 shoutouts by the NimbleElephant123s of the world.
