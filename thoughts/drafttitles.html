First Things First: Do My Titles Suck?

My guess is yes70%. My naming them was never optimized for maximum engagement—I just wanted something simple. I was also incentivized to keep it short so my subtitle/description could be longer, as I have a rule that posts could only take up a single line on my previous index page.

So to test my theory, I made a CSV of all of my titles and subtitles (or data-description attributes in each a href tag in the index file) at the time of writing and fed them into some LLMs and the minds of a few friends to get their thoughts with the simple prompt of:

Column A is a title and column B is the a subtitle. Which column is better?

Robotic Thoughts

(I paraphrase for length purposes.)

Claude Opus 4.8 is straightforward:

Subtitles. Your subtitles have a consistent voice and your titles don't. ... the subtitle column reads like you — funny, specific, opinionated ... The title column reads like a table of contents someone else generated from your posts.

Gemini 3.5 Flash is a bit more cautious about insulting my title-naming abilities:

If you are judging purely on literary intrigue and engagement, Column B is better. It contains the actual "meat" of the ideas, the voice of the writer, and the emotional hook that makes a reader want to keep reading.

GPT 5.6 Sol breaks the tie and chooses titles:

Column A is stronger overall. It has more personality, specificity, and curiosity. Column B works best when it clarifies A without repeating it. The strongest subtitles add just enough context to tell the reader what the piece is about. Main issue with B: many entries feel functional rather than polished—some restate the title, while others read like notes or outlines.

What Makes a Good Title Anyway?

It's catchy. Engaging. Makes some passerby stop their scrolling or thumb from going over the X button. Elicits some strong reaction in either good or bad ways. Not clickbait. Funny (at times). Informative and descriptive. Rage-inducing? Depends on who you ask.

It also depends on the piece itself. Is it meant to be raw info in a repository-like format? Boring is fine and likely good to get the information across. Is it a strong claim that readers should respond to? Put that assertion in the title. Is it a reflection? Be vulnerable and open about it from the get-go.

How I'm Title-ing From Here On Out

I'll be shifting everything up one level. My main title will get discarded, the subtitle will get promoted, and the subsubtitle, if at all, will be generated from the deep, dark depths of my keyboard.
