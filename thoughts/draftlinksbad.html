Links Posts Made Me a Goodharter and a Boring Person

Published

Quality over quantity.

Contents

I read a lot on the internet—3600 links and counting over 6.5 years. I'm guessing I'm around the 90-95th percentile of curiosity, so finding all these interesting, unique, fun, weird, awesome links is an easy, free, and great way to satisfy that.

Documenting them also feels great. Like I'm making progress on something. Like I'm building out and growing my second brain. Like I'm becoming smarter and more interesting and more well-read. And so I kept documenting.

I started out including both descriptions/thoughts and tags on each link. The former was to help me prove my comprehension and think about the piece, the latter so I could quickly reference a topic (this was well before I even considered creating a filterable table or something; I've also since deleted the tags as I didn't find them that useful). It was around 2021 and 2022 that my descriptions and thoughts began to wane, becoming more succinct and factual than wordy and opinionated. And in late 2022 I stopped the descriptions altogether, telling myself that they weren't adding that much value and sheer volume was more important. After all, even if you can't remember the meals you've eaten, they've made you.

Turns out that idea is useless. Damn you, Emerson, for sounding so wise and cool and making me believe that shit.

The Negative Impact

Documenting my links in the way that I have has been a net negative for my life in the following ways:

  • Long: I'm less inclined to read longer or more challenging texts because they're a much larger investment for the same payout, i.e., a line in the links file. Why spend 30 minutes reading something when I can spend 5, or even 1?
  • Metrics: I'm more likely to speed read through the piece to maximize my links per time metric. I no longer engage with the piece or any of the ideas around it; my eyes simply glaze over the words as I feign understanding. (I randomly chose 5 links and only strongly remember the content of 3 of them. 60% seems fairly low here, whereas if I had really engaged and written some thoughts I)
  • Opportunity: Was this reading really all for naught? Did I gain anything? What would I have been doing otherwise? Browsing social media? Or writing or exercising? I'd like to think something more productive than just reading words that went in one eye and out the other.
  • Thinking: My thinking and analytical skills have decreased over time, and I suspect this is a major part of this. The brain is a muscle and must be trained regularly, including engaging with new ideas.

Moving Forward

In my effort to be a good citizen of the internet, I'm implementing a new rule for myself: I can only add a link to my links page if I've thoroughly engaged with it. I'll allocate around a minimum of 5 minutes for engagement and ensure I post any thoughts to the link's comment section, if present.
