Self-Exclusion For Harmful Purchases

Published

Contents

Nevada gaming places offer self-exclusion (https://www.nvsep.org/), the ability to voluntarily say “I do not want to be allowed to gamble anymore. Please prevent me from doing so in the future.” No matter how the casinos design their addictive labyrinths or the companies their slot machines or X, the self-excluded cannot bypass this feature (although there may be way?) [ask Claude about bypassing]

There should be more self-exclusion options available for people to sign up for, especially around purchases. The exclusion can exist at many levels:

  1. identification level: person with ID number X is not allowed to purchase, as determined by the business scanning the ID card before potentially-qualifying purchases
  2. credit/debit card level: card ending in 1234 is not allowed to purchase. Any new cards ordered would come with the same restrictions, so in effect this would be a more narrow version of the ID level.
  3. Time period: I don’t want to spend more than X in Y period

Alternatively, for the exclusion-wary, they may elect to be taxed more heavily for qualifying purchases. The tax takes place at purchase and can be directed to the charity of their choice [IS THIS BAD]. For example, Emily may now pay $100 for the $10 case of beer, where the $90 goes towards helping wannabe Internet writers implement their societal system ideas that would make the world a better place but create weird, perverse, difficult-to-detect incentives that would only reveal themselves after some time.

List of Qualifying Purchases

- Drugs: alcohol, nicotine, marijuana - Pornography - Gambling: adding new money to betting websites - Any addictive or harmful behavior

Getting Out

Getting in should be as easy as possible for those that want to get in; getting out should be as hard as possible for those that want to get out. But there exists the risk of being pressured, bullied, forced, or mistaken into entering this agreement. “Haha bro it’d be so funny if I could never buy X again” says Tyler at 3:00am after eight drinks with his buddies, only to realize in horror that, in fact, that wasn’t very funny and now getting out is incredibly difficult. We are all adults who live with consequences, but we also change with life experiences and a problem before may no longer be a problem later. We live under the threat of pressure and bullying and coercion for other situations.
