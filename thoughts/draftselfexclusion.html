Stopping People From Doing "Bad" Things

Nevada gaming places offer self-exclusion (https://www.nvsep.org/), the ability to voluntarily say something to the effect of “Please prevent me from gambling in the future.” No matter how the casinos design their addictive labyrinths of addiction and hell and depravity or the morally-corrupt companies program their slot machines or app algorithms, the self-excluded cannot bypass this feature. [although there may be way? ask Claude about bypassing]

This is a beautiful mechanism and there should be more self-exclusion options (i.e., commitment devices) available for people to sign up for.

The exclusion can be set at many levels:

  1. Identification: Person with ID number X is excluded from purchasing Y, as determined by the business scanning the ID card before qualifying purchases. For example, Evan is an alcoholic that excluded himself from buying alcohol on his path to sobriety. When the liquor store scans his ID, they see that he's over 21 but on the exclusion list, so they deny the sale.
  2. Credit/debit card: Card number X is not allowed to purchase Y. Any new cards ordered would come with the same restrictions, so in effect this would be a more narrow version of the ID level.
  3. Time period: I don’t want to spend more than $X in Y period. This would be set up at the credit card company level.
  4. Merchant type: Merchants are roughly categorized by what they sell. Companies could standardize and granularize these types further, then allow the user to self-exclude at the card level for types of purchases.
  5. SKU: [IS THERE A WAY TO HAVE A CATCH-ALL FOR E.G. REESE'S CUPS OR SIMILAR?]

Alternatively, for those wary of complete exclusion, they may elect to be taxed more heavily for qualifying purchases. The tax takes place at purchase and can be directed to the charity of their choice or just go straight to the government as normal. For example, Travis may now pay $100 for a case of beer that's normally $10, with the additional $90 going towards helping wannabe internet writers implement their societal system ideas that would make the world a better place but create weird, perverse, difficult-to-detect incentives that only reveal themselves after some time of being in use.

The Self-Exclusion Commitment Device

Here's the process for signing up:

  1. Candidate goes to the respective governing body of exclusion.
    • For age-limited items, it's the government. For non-age-limited items, it's their bank or credit card company.
    • For basic or low-cost items, it can be done online with some ID checks. For serious or high-cost items, it must be done in person to confirm the person's willingness.
  2. Candidate fills out form with conditions of exclusion. Is it always or sometimes? Any exceptions? What are the exit conditions?
  3. The governing body adds them to the relevant database that is updated on a regular basis.
  4. Done! The candidate will now get rejected when trying to purchase said item or category of item.

To get the ban lifted, they must satisfy the exit conditions they specified during the initial sign-up.

Concerns

Being Pressured to Exclude

Addicts are often pressured (for good reason!) to get help for their issue. Family members may do the same here by pressuring an alcoholic to self-exclude from purchasing alcohol. It should ultimately be up to the individual if they want to exclude themselves, requiring a robust method of initial election ("I want to add myself to this list and nobody is forcing me to") and ongoing election/ ("I want to stay on this list and nobody is forcing me to").

When signing up, there will be legal protections for the candidate to ensure they aren't pressured into anything.

The Difficulty of Getting Out

Getting in should be accessible for those that want to get in; getting out should be as difficult as the person wants it to be when initially signing up. We all live with the consequences of our actions, but we also change with experiences and a problem now before may no longer be a problem later. Or our future selves may really want to get out later but be happy our past selves made it difficult. Or they may be really upset when they want to buy that pretty girl at the bar a drink but have to settle for a mocktail because they can't buy alcohol. Choose your exit conditions wisely!

Take the scenario where Josh says “bro it’d be so funny if I could never buy X again” at 3:00am after eight drinks, only to realize in horror that, in fact, that wasn’t very funny and now getting out is incredibly difficult. This risk is mitigated by pre-defining the severity levels of each type of exclusion and requiring in-person attendance for certain situations. That said, plenty of other, arguably more harmful, actions exist in the world that don't check for sobriety or sanity. I can buy the dumbest financial options known to man or something ridiculous on eBay with a no return policy without anyone batting an eye or checking if I'm in a good state of mind. A similar expectation can be set here.

Qualifying Purchases

Here's a non-exhaustive list of potential purchases and categories:

  • Drugs: Alcohol, nicotine, marijuana. Sadly I don't think a lot of (hard) drug dealers would be willing to abide by this idea!
  • Pornography (yes, I know some states already have a ban on 18+ users viewing certain sites and that arguably causes harm)
  • Gambling: Adding new money to betting websites; being allowed into casinos; purchasing lottery tickets
  • Food and drink: Unhealthy foods. What if foods' SKUs had associated nutrition info and the block was at the macronutrient level?
