Foreword

I am not calling for violence against any one person or group. My intent is to explain what I believe is a real possibility in the coming years.

Abstract

Artificial intelligence (AI) will reduce both the number of available jobs and the value of human labor (despite regular reassurances of a prosperous future from industry leaders). The AI need not be AGI nor ASI—there is plenty of labor that can be fully or partially automated, enabling greater productivity or lower cost of unit output per employee.

A rogue, low-skilled, laid-off worker who believes they have no path forward or someone acting on behalf of an organization whose jobs are at stake are the most likely to attempt an assassination against an AI leader. Contributing factors include:

  • How much meaning their job provided them. Those who derive great senses of purpose from their occupations are more likely to commit violent acts if it gets taken away from them.
  • Their ethical belief system. Are they utilitarians who try to reason that AI will do more harm than good? Deontologists who refuse to kill despite the possible personal harm they stand to experience?
  • Their ability to calculate the cost-benefit of the murder, whether accurate or inaccurate. If the calculation comes back to find that AI is net bad for the world and there's a chance to stop it or slow it down, some may find that they want to be the hero.
  • Society's general sentiment towards AI at the time. If AI is being demonized, then a murderer may see themselves as being lauded by others after the kill.

Assassination Examples

First, let's examine examples of political assassinations that were successful not (only) in killing the target, but in creating the effect the assassin wanted, the ultimate goal. These case studies may provide evidence to potential assassins that assassinations actually do work, contrary to popular belief.

Technological Job Displacement Examples

Next, let's

Artists

From the abstract of Demirci et al.'s Who Is AI Replacing? The Impact of Generative AI on Online Freelancing Platforms:

Our findings indicate a 21% decrease in the number of job posts for automation-prone jobs related to writing and coding, compared to jobs requiring manual-intensive skills, within eight months after the introduction of ChatGPT. We show that the reduction in the number of job posts increases competition among freelancers while the remaining automation-prone jobs are of greater complexity and offer higher pay. We also find that the introduction of Image-generating AI technologies led to a 17% decrease in the number of job posts related to image creation.

Liu et al. find similar results in their “Generate” the Future of Work through AI: Empirical Evidence from Online Labor Markets:

we uncover a post-ChatGPT decline in labor demand, supply, and transactions for submarkets pertaining to text-related and programming-related jobs, in comparison to those not directly exposed to ChatGPT’s core functionalities

As well as Yuan & Chen's The Impact of ChatGPT Launch on the Demand for Content Generation Services in the Freelancing Market:

Our investigation reveals a significant decrease in demand for human content generation services following the ChatGPT launch. ... Notably, we observe a significant increase in demand for idea planning services, while demand for text reviewing services and services provided by human writers with a PhD degree remains unaffected by the launch of ChatGPT.

Crowd workers are also using LLMs to improve productivity as found by Veselovsky et al.'s Artificial Artificial Artificial Intelligence: Crowd Workers Widely Use Large Language Models for Text Production Tasks (keep in mind that agentic AI is well on its way, and combining with an LLM makes for a potent combination that can easily displace these crowd workers):

estimate that 33–46% of crowd workers used LLMs when completing the task

The people affected are both low and high skill, and to some correlation, low and high intelligence. The lows are unlikely to find work, whereas the highs can more easily pivot to a different field.

Potential Targets

Potential targets can be graded on a variety of factors (listed in no particular order):

  • Impact on AI development (if killed, will it greatly impact AI timelines?)
  • Insulation from the plights of average people (it is much easier to kill a wealthy person who won't feel the effects of their work)
  • Difficulty of assassination (the more difficult the job, the less likely it is to be carried out)
  • Cooling effects (if killed, how much will others intentionally slow down to avoid a similar fate?)
  • Sheer dislikability (do they lie, cheat, steal, etc?)
  • Recognition
