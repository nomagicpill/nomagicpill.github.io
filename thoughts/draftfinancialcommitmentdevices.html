Using Willpower is Unwise

Do you really think that using willpower is a wise choice? That it's like a muscle you can grow? That the daily/weekly/monthly/yearly/lifetime reservoir won't run dry eventually, leaving you to drag your feet and rely on caffeine and Adderall to carry you to the finish line? That there aren't better things you could apply it to? That there aren't more efficient, easier methods out there of getting things done? That it makes you look cooler or stronger or better? That it somehow makes your life better?

Money as a Fire Under Your Ass

Money continues to be the ultimate commitment device: the likelihood that the task gets done scales proportionally with the amount of money on the line; the amount of money to stake is effectively infinite; the types of contracts and amount of them one can enter is infinite; proof can be required to eliminate lying.

And yet most people, even the ones who know about them, don't use them. Why?

It can feel weird to say "I'll give up $20 if I don't call my mom by Saturday" because it implies the phone call is worth $20, and if the bettor doesn't call, then she definitely isn't worth $20–sorry, Mom! That feels especially shitty, so avoiding the commitment avoids the chance of that happening altogether.

Or it makes the person feel weak, like they have to threaten themselves in order to do something, regardless of whether or not they want to do it. I often want to call my mom and know it's the right thing to do, but my life can get busy and to-do list long, so staking some money on it ensures I prioritize it the appropriate amount, else it may fall by the wayside via excuses or forgetfulness or exhaustion.

Or the thing that they claim is so important to them (and thus would be a perfect candidate for a well-written contract) isn't actually as important as they thought upon further consideration, so they avoid committing anything. Revealing preferences to oneself and others can also be scary. But isn't there something that's important enough to put money on?

While these are all understandable reasons, they are still blockers to the goal being achieved in a more likely-to-be-successful and efficient manner and should be resolved. [FINISH]
