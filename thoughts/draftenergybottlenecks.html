Bottlenecks

Every person, process, and goal has a bottleneck somewhere in the line because not everything can is equal. The pursuit of eliminating bottlenecks essentially just shifts it to a different spot in the line—when capacity in one area outgrows capacity in another, the latter becomes bottlenecked.

Certain things should not be bottlenecks depending on the area; if they are, then measures should be taken to remove it quickly. Time and money are often the bottleneck in growing a startup, as evident by the lack of sleep and (hopefully multiple!) funding rounds the founders go through on their path to glory and riches, both of which are the employees' measures to relieve the time and money constraints. If they had access to the mythical infinite time and money glitches, then scaling would be easy. But alas, those don't exist, so they're stuck doing things the old-fashioned way.

(This is a blatant message to you, the reader, to think deeply about your current bottlenecks in life and what you are doing about them, if anything.)

Energy Bottlenecks Are Especially Bad

Lacking energy to do things despite knowing what to do and wanting to do it is terrible. There's a lingering fear that doing the thing while motivation and energy are low will breed resentment towards it. And you want to do the thing, or at least want to want to do it, but just can't.

Energy bottlenecks can be reduced in a similar way to time and money.
