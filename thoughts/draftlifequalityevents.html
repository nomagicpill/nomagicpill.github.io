Manufacturing Quality Events

Manufacturing companies are all about churning out as much product as possible as fast as possible for as cheap as possible. Obviously some of these goals are at odds with each other, leading to the infamous triangle that managers refuse to overtly believe in:

It's all about finding a balance. But sometimes the balance exists at the expense of the quality of the product, unbeknownst to the factory, leading to a few potential scenarios:

  1. Having a near-miss and mini-heart attack at what could have happened
  2. Having to throw out product. This is essentially wasted time and effort towards making the material. The further it is in its life cycle, the costlier the event.
  3. Having a customer return. This is both wasted time and effort and a massive blow to the trust the customer has in the company. If it's bad enough, the customer may choose someone else.

Gradations exist between those three, although the message is clear: don't make low-quality products!

But sometimes shit happens and we have to throw away a gagillion widgets. What happens then?

Root Cause Analysis

Root cause analysis (RCA) is split up into four distinct root causes (RC), not all of which are applicable to every event and some of which there are multiple of each. First, start with a problem statement, then work down to the individual RCs of:

  • Non-conformance technical root cause (NCTRC): What was wrong that caused the problem to happen? Fixing this will prevent the specific problem from happening again.
  • Non-conformance systemic root cause (NCSRC): Why was the problem "allowed" to happen? Fixing this will prevent future problems from happening again.
  • Non-detection technical root cause (NDTRC): Why wasn't the problem detected immediately? Fixing this will allow improved detection for next time.
  • Non-detection systemic root cause (NDSRC): Why was the problem "allowed" to not be detected? Fixing this will ensure future problems are detected.

In sum, the technical root causes are why the problem happened and the systemic root causes are what allowed the problem to happen. Once all of the root causes are identified, actions can be taken to fix each.

For example, here's the RCA for when semiconductor chips are thrown away because they had too many particles on them:

  • NCTRC: The machine's part had reached the end of its life (EOL) and was no longer functioning properly
    • Action: Replace the part
  • NCSRC: The specific part did not have a replacement schedule
    • Action: Define a replacement schedule such that the part doesn't reach EOL
  • NDTRC: There is no existing way to detect the part reaching EOL
    • Action: Set up a monitoring system to detect when it reaches or is close to reaching EOL, just in case it's sooner than the replacement schedule
  • NDSRC: N/A

Analzying the Root Causes of Your Life's Problems

RCA is a wonderful system in that it allows mistakes to be learned from and gaps fixed, shoring up the dam separating problems and a smooth, problem-free life. It forces one to consider everything, to go down each path and ask why this or that happened, how they let it happen, and what they have to do to make sure it never happens again.

But let's be real, nobody wants to go through the dreaded RCA rigmarole every time something bad happens.

This is why I propose that an abbreviated RCA should be used in someone's everyday life to address things when they have quality(-of-life) events. When something goes wrong, don't just accept it or move on—learn from it! Understand it! Ask questions about it! Fix it!

The questions are almost identical to the NXXRC questions from above:

  1. Has the problem been fixed? This is the immediate patch to the problem that prevents more damage from happening.
  2. How will future problems identical to this be prevented? This is the long-term patch that will prevent similar problems from occurring in the future.
  3. What can be done to detect the problem sooner? This helps to highlight and eliminate detection gaps.

Here are a few simple examples to show how helpful LRCA can be across multiple domains of life. They're not in the (NC/ND)(TRC/SRC) format, but the point still gets across.

Andrew missed his flight because:

  • Traffic was worse than normal
  • The security checkpoint was longer than normal
  • He lost track of time before leaving for the airport

Now to answer the questions:

  1. Has the problem been fixed?
    • Yes. Andrew has rebooked his flight.
  2. How will future problems identical to this be prevented?
    • Schedule a rideshare in advance, preferably at the time of flight booking. If that's too early, set a reminder at the time of booking to do so as soon as the window opens up.
    • Establish a personal rule to arrive at the airport at least two hours in advance of the flight departure time. This creates a cushion in case of extra long times during the drive or security.
    • Purchase a TSA PreCheck membership to get through security faster
  3. What can be done to detect the problem sooner?
    • Set up automatic reminders to check traffic and TSA wait times X hours before the flight

Celia didn't get promoted at work because:

  • She didn't get regular feedback from her boss about her performance
  • She didn't work high-profile projects
  • She was disagreeable with other members of the group
  1. Has the problem been fixed?
    • There's not an immediate problem to fix. Celia will have to wait until the next promotion cycle, but can and should start on other actions in the meantime.
  2. How will future problems identical to this be prevented?
    • Set up regular performance reviews and hold both parties accountable for attending
    • Push to work on high-profile projects. This means actively seeking them out, proposing them herself, and asking to get put on already-existing teams.
    • Be more friendly with everyone in the office
  3. What can be done to detect the problem sooner?
    • Set up regular performance reviews and hold both parties accountable for attending
