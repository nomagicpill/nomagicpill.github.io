Day 1: Tacos

Tacos honestly sum up Thursday night. Besides the post-flight-in-the-airport-parking-lot obligatory shotguns with a combo of hard kombucha and IPAs. But those were already a given. Tacos El Gordo was fire—authentically Mexican with plenty of cilantro and onions on top of tender, juicy carne asada or al pastor. A massive line on a Thursday evening at 10 is the mark of a solid spot and it didn't disappoint.

The drive in to the city didn't feel like most of the other major U.S. cities (NYC, Boston, Chicago); it didn't loom in the distance while never getting any bigger no matter how quickly or long you drove, but instead came up quickly, which matched the general feel: small, cozy, and tucked among suburbs of coastal California.

Day 2: Sun

Most people—myself provably included—associate UV strength with heat. If it's hot, the UV level must be high; if it's low, UV is low. San Diego will chew you up and spit you out with red and peeling skin if this is something you believe and act on. Temp was pushing a comfy, wind-assisted 80 °F with a very sneaky UV of 8. Laying out in the sun without sunscreen was not a good idea. Anyway, the picture is painted.

After a quick gym sesh we made sure to cancel out the calories burned with donuts from Donut Bar & Bakery, coffee from Mostra, and chilaquiles from Cocina 35. San Diego had more Mexican influence than I remember (although I was 10).
