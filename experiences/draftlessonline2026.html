LessOnline trip report from 05-07 June 2026.

A List of My Thoughts

Man, the people who come here are really fucking cool. And interesting. And weird. Ambitious. Risk tolerant. Caring and thoughtful. Welcoming and warm. Passionate. Fun! Funny. The attendance, like LessWrong readership and authorship, is of impeccable quality. Frontier AI lab employees mingle with hedge fund founders, startup founders, and cinematographers during dinner; college students talks with software engineers and non-profit employees and teachers late into the night. Everyone has their own interests that they share passionately with others while reciprocrating the energy back when listening. Curiosity connects us all through the atmosphere, questions we ask, and behaviors we practice. The community feels alive at all times of the day. Some conversations are focused on business, picking the brains of people they normally wouldn't have this much direct, unfettered access to normally. Others revolve around esoteric or niche topics, chosen for those reasons and the fact that they can't be had elsewhere with the same depth or excitement. Others revolve around AI, the perennial topic that cannot be escaped (nor should it be). This trip AGI-pilled me in its mere three-day duration. [FINISH AGI PILLEDNESS] You can find people gathered around in an effort to experience novel qualia: holographic chocolate, the thermal grill illusion, feeling like their arms are sinking through the floor. Some attendees walk around in bird jackets, kindly explaining they represent the great grey shrike, a bird that impales its dead prey on thorns or barbed wire to store or tear apart. Bouncing from conversation to conversation is expected, even encouraged—there is only so much time available (interpret that in both ways) that one shouldn't waste it on bad conversations or spend too much time because diminishing returns exist. Sessions are hosted by enthusiastic speakers wanting to share their thoughts or experience with the group. Rooms are often packed, some sold out, some more desolate due to the...specificity. There's something for everyone. Sometimes too much. Slots in both time and location are limited and force attendees to choose and consider opportunity cost, weighing session this versus session that version conversation this versus conversation that. They decide at some point, rarely disappointed, but always happy in the moment, or so the smiles and shine in their eyes say.
