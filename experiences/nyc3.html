New York City III

New York City trip report from 17-21 April 2025.

The city—the city—has lost its luster, the sheen that kept me enamored for the cumulative 100+ hours I had spent inside its concrete labrinyth decorated with lights, echoing a cachophony of voices and car horns, emanating a miasma of subway and urine and trash between the door-open shops letting their freshly-made food waft into the open street. The city beckoned to me from afar, a dream of years past and relationships thriving; a time of easiness and novelty and excitement; a longing for more and better. And yet, none of that was there, at least not in the same intensity that drew me in previously. The city was still its same vibrant self in all the ways I had remembered, but the elevated experience wrongly became the expectation as most things do. Novelty, and thus infatuation, can only last for so long before it subsides into a deeper enjoyment of-sorts, one that isn't so jumpy but rather mellow and steady.

The people remain the focal point of the city. Anybody who talks about the things to do in the city before they talk about the people is either mistaken or wrong: the people make the things to do. From the subway zombies vomiting in others' bags to nice Dominicans running bakeries to the 100s of IB VPs to the elderly Chinese men chatting away on a sunny Sunday morning to the many book clubs in the park, these are the ones who (mostly) bring the craziness, for better or worse.

So many people I've met who live in NYC talk about the FOMO they experience.
