My Current Status

I'm currently a regular at four different restaurants: RHC, BPP, B, and TYT. All of the owners recognize me as soon as I walk in the door and know my standard order. I'm fairly confident that each of them would let me pay another day if I forgot my wallet.

My method of getting to this point was straightforward: be kind, be consistent, and stand out. Starting out I'd go 2-4 times a week to spam them with the sight of my face in hopes of making it stick. It did. I used an odd order name that they had probably never heard before and made them do a double-take and verify they heard me correctly. They did. Kindness needs no explanation.

This prompts the question: what's the formula for becoming a regular? Things like how many employees work there, how invested they are in the business, how many customers they have, how often do you have to go, how much do you have to buy, what do you have to say, etc.

Benefits

First, some tangible benefits I've gotten include straight up free extra food (full pizza instead of a few slices, extra chicken tenders) or priority in getting my order made quickly.

But the benefit that I think most people underestimate or don't even consider is the sense of giving that becoming a regular provides. Now I'm a believer that you should rarely, if ever, choose to not get the best thing out of some sense of obligation, whether to the owner, where the product is made, etc. It's competition that makes the product—in this case food—better. If the local pizza joint makes shitty pizza then no one will come, no matter how kind or charismatic the owner is.

The key here is finding a local joint (basically a requirement if you want to become a regular) serving up delicious food that you can regularly support. A few things happen in turn. First, you get to eat regularly eat delicious food. Second, you're contributing to and ensuring other people get to eat delicious food. My Future Status, or Experiments

First, what's the strategy besides the kind/consistent/stand out approach? A family-owned restaurant is a must. Employees work most days, increasing face time and the chance they remember you. Going at uncrowded times also ensures you stand out a bit more instead of just being another face in the crowd.
