Context

Why All the Data?

Fabs are incredibly hungry for data. Insatiably hungry. Data helps to connect patterns, solve problems, troubleshoot issues, and just plan understand.

What is the Data?

How much data could one fab possibly need? And what could they possibly be measuring that requires terabytes of data?

Let's find out! Here's a "short" list of potential signals fabs can keep track of, binned out into individual tool components. For example, engineers may want to keep track of the temperature, pressure, and power of component1.

  • Temperature
  • Pressure
  • Angle
  • Distance
  • Position
  • Voltage
  • Current
  • Resistance
  • Power
  • Quantity
  • Status

Now those are just single characteristics that don't add up to much on their own. But what happens when we take measurements across multiple components across multiple tools across the entire fab? A nice Fermi approximation is in store!

Let's assume the following for the NMP fab:

  • 1000 tools
  • 500 signals per tool
  • 500 kB data per signal per hour (empirical data at a collection frequency of 1 Hz; this includes the context needed to interpret the data)

The math is then pretty easy:

total = 1000 tools × 500 signals/tool × 500 kB/signal/hour = 250 GB per hour ≈ 2000 TB per year of data

But wait! Those are just raw signals that the tool is reporting to the fab data monster. There are still plenty more sources of data:

  • Derived values of data: mean, median, standard deviation, etc
